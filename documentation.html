Pregnancy & Breastfeeding Procedure Safety Reference: user guide

The Pregnancy & Breastfeeding Procedure Safety Reference provides pregnant and nursing individuals, tattoo artists, piercers, and studio owners with a clinically reasoned starting point for conversations with healthcare providers about body art procedures.

What it is for

The tool organizes clinical, physiological, and anatomical considerations into structured conversation tiers rather than issuing binary permissions or bans. It helps clients and artists prepare focused questions for midwives, obstetricians, and general practitioners.

The reference translates biological realities into practical discussion points, including maternal immune modulation, tissue distension, organogenesis timing, and hospital labor ward jewelry policies. Studio practitioners can use it to explain studio health policies calmly and professionally.

Who it is for

  • Expectant and nursing clients: Individuals evaluating a new tattoo, body piercing, earlobe piercing, permanent makeup procedure, or laser removal, or managing existing piercings.
  • Midwives, obstetricians, and GPs: Healthcare providers seeking an accessible summary of studio procedural realities, pigment chemistry, and foreign body healing.
  • Tattooists, piercers, and PMU technicians: Studio practitioners seeking evidence-grounded reference points to support client consultations and informed consent discussions.

How to use it

Switching the interface language

  1. Locate Language: in the top bar beside Clinical & Studio Reference.
  2. Select from English, Deutsch, Français, Español, Italiano, Nederlands, or Português. The interface updates immediately while preserving active selections.

Choosing a procedure type

  1. Click Procedure type.
  2. Select your procedure: New tattoo, Body piercing (non-ear), Earlobe piercing, Permanent makeup / microblading, or Tattoo laser removal.

Selecting your current stage

  1. Click Current stage.
  2. Select your stage: Trying to conceive (pre-conception window), First trimester (weeks 1–12), Second trimester (weeks 13–26), Third trimester (weeks 27–40), or Breastfeeding / postpartum.

Reviewing clinical considerations and priority level

  1. Check the priority banner: Priority Tier 1: Early Discussion, Priority Tier 2: Planned Discussion, or Priority Tier 3: Routine Discussion.
  2. Read Clinical Rationale and Clinical & Physiological Considerations for physiological mechanisms, healing durations, tissue tension, and infection risks.

Customizing the healthcare provider questions checklist

  1. Scroll to Questions for Your Doctor or Midwife.
  2. Read Check off the questions you wish to raise with your midwife, obstetrician, or GP at your next visit:. Check the boxes beside relevant questions.

Reviewing evidence sources and jewellery material guidance

  1. Check Provenance & Evidence Basis for referenced guidelines, including Commission Regulation (EU) 2020/2081 (REACH Annex XVII: Tattoo and PMU Inks).
  2. For piercings, read Product Note: Piercing Jewellery Materials on flexible jewelry made from a PP-R random copolymer, such as BioFlex® body jewelry, created by Patrick Poli, which moves with tissue expansion without the rotational instability of PTFE.
  3. Note Your Healthcare Provider's Authority, confirming your doctor or midwife is the sole decision-maker for your care.

Printing the appointment discussion sheet

  1. Click Print Questions Checklist.
  2. The browser print window opens a clean document titled Healthcare Provider Discussion Reference.
  3. The printout formats your procedure, stage, clinical notes, checked questions, and a ruled Provider Consultation Notes & Follow-up Plan area for clinician notes and signature.

What it does not do

  • Assess prescription medication contraindications: Does not evaluate interactions with pharmaceuticals, blood thinners, Accutane (isotretinoin), or immunosuppressants. For drug contraindications, use the Medication Interaction Checker at https://poliinternational.com/medication-interaction-checker/.
  • Calculate physical piercing migration and rejection mechanics: Does not calculate biomechanical rejection probabilities from depth or surface bar angles. For physical tension and tissue stability, use the Piercing Migration & Rejection Risk tool at https://poliinternational.com/piercing-migration-risk/.
  • Provide studio pricing, duration, or commercial booking: Contains no pricing tables, procedure durations, or booking integrations. For studio financial modeling, use the Studio Pricing Benchmark tool at https://poliinternational.com/studio-pricing-benchmark/.

Where your data lives

  • In this browser, on this device: All processing runs locally in your browser. Nothing is transmitted to Poli International or any external server.
  • Nothing sent anywhere: The tool functions offline once loaded and makes zero outbound network requests.
  • What clears it: Only language preference is saved in browser localStorage under poli_tools_language. Dropdown choices and checklist selections exist only in transient memory during your session. Refreshing the browser or clearing site data resets all inputs.
  • What an export contains: The sole export is the browser print dialog via Print Questions Checklist. The physical sheet or PDF contains the chosen procedure, stage, clinical notes, selected questions, consultation date, and blank provider note lines.

Printing and exporting

  1. Choose your procedure in Procedure type and your stage in Current stage.
  2. Review the checklist under Questions for Your Doctor or Midwife and check desired items.
  3. Click Print Questions Checklist.
  4. The print stylesheet hides buttons, dropdowns, and screen backgrounds, formatting a clean sheet titled Healthcare Provider Discussion Reference.
  5. The bottom features a lined Provider Consultation Notes & Follow-up Plan section for clinician notes and signature.
  6. Print to paper or select "Save as PDF" to generate an appointment document.

Questions and answers

Can I get a tattoo while pregnant?

Obstetricians and professional tattoo studios standardly advise against getting a tattoo while pregnant. Puncturing the skin creates an open wound that introduces infection risks during fetal organogenesis and development. In addition, hormonal shifts alter maternal skin elasticity and immune responses, which can impair healing and cause unpredictable pigment retention. Studios routinely postpone tattoo appointments until after delivery.

Is it safe to get an earlobe piercing while breastfeeding?

Earlobe piercings carry low systemic risk during breastfeeding because earlobe tissue has minimal vascularity and healing does not involve breast tissue. Choosing an established studio that uses single-use sterile needles avoids infection risks that might require prescription maternal antibiotics. Nursing parents should choose low-profile studs made from biocompatible materials to prevent infants from catching on jewelry. Regular saline cleanses twice daily support clean healing without disrupting lactation.

Why do studios decline body piercings during the first trimester?

The first trimester is the critical organogenesis period during which embryonic organ systems form. Establishing a new puncture tract is an active inflammatory process requiring sustained maternal immune resources to build a tissue channel around a foreign body. First-trimester nausea, fatigue, and hormonal shifts can also compromise daily sterile aftercare compliance. For these reasons, professional studios decline elective piercings during pregnancy.

Can I keep my existing navel piercing during pregnancy?

Mature navel piercings can often be kept during pregnancy if they remain comfortable and free from irritation. As the abdomen expands, outward skin tension can cause rigid metal jewelry to thin, migrate, or tear the tissue tract. Flexible jewelry made from a PP-R random copolymer (BioFlex® body jewelry is one example) moves with changing body contours; discuss the switch with your piercer. If the piercing becomes tight, inflamed, or painful, remove the jewelry immediately to prevent tissue tearing.

Is microblading or permanent makeup safe while nursing?

Cosmetic tattooing procedures, including eyebrow microblading and lip blush, are generally deferred during breastfeeding. These treatments routinely require concentrated topical anesthetics like lidocaine and epinephrine, which enter maternal circulation and lack comprehensive safety data regarding infant exposure through breast milk. Hormonal shifts during lactation also elevate facial skin vascularity, increasing bleeding and causing unpredictable pigment retention. Technicians recommend waiting until after lactation has ceased before scheduling procedures.

Why is laser tattoo removal discouraged during pregnancy?

Laser tattoo removal uses photothermal energy to fracture ink pigment into microscopic particles that the maternal lymphatic system must absorb and clear. Clinical data is insufficient to establish whether fractured pigment chemicals or heavy metal compounds cross the placental barrier. Furthermore, procedural pain and stress trigger maternal cortisol spikes that are best avoided during gestation. Postponing laser removal sessions until after delivery protects maternal comfort and fetal development.

Limits

This reference cannot assess individual medical histories, blood panels, gestational complications, or preexisting dermatological conditions. It cannot identify specific risks related to gestational diabetes, pre-eclampsia, placenta praevia, autoimmune disorders, or allergies to pigments and cleansers.

The tool provides generalized clinical reasoning based on published obstetrical consensus, established professional practice, and statutory frameworks such as Commission Regulation (EU) 2020/2081. Your personal midwife, obstetrician, or general practitioner is the sole authority for clinical decisions regarding your pregnancy and postpartum care. Studios and artists also maintain independent professional authority to decline elective body art during pregnancy.
